SLATE

a clock-synced color slate — adisinghania.com/slate

What it does

Every second, the screen flips to the next color in a fixed 8-color cycle, driven purely by wall-clock time (Date.now()). Any two devices with NTP-synced clocks — which iPads, Macs, and phones keep automatically — show the same color at the same instant, no network connection between them needed.

How to sync two cameras

  1. Open this page on a screen near each camera (or on each recording device itself). Two different devices are fine — that's the point.
  2. Roll all cameras and make sure each one sees a slate in frame for a few seconds. Then shoot normally.
  3. In your editor, find the same color flip in both clips (e.g. the frame where it turns RED at epoch N — the epoch counter in frame tells you which second is which) and align the clips there.
  4. For sub-second precision: the corner squares flash white for the first 120 ms of every second, and the sweep bar crosses one ruler notch per 100 ms.

Audio sync

Turn on tick to emit a short click at every second boundary (higher pitch on multiples of ten). If your cameras record sound, you can align clips on the waveform instead of — or as a check against — the color flips.

Trust, but verify

clock? estimates this device's clock offset against the web server's clock. It's coarse (±0.5 s) — treat it as a sanity check that the clock isn't wildly off, not as a calibration. Real NTP-synced devices agree to within a few tens of milliseconds, i.e. about one frame.
